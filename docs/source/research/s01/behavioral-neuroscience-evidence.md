# Behavioral, neuroscience, and psychology evidence for the standard

## Scope and epistemic rule

This is evidence to guide a personal-system design, not a diagnosis, treatment plan, or a way to turn a person into a deterministic controller. the agent should use it to make helpful options easier, retain human agency, and learn from outcomes. It must not infer motivation, health status, or character from a proxy.

**Evidence labels:** **robust** means a synthesis or replicated programme supports the mechanism; **context-dependent** means effect sizes, persistence, or applicability vary materially; **unsupported/unsafe** identifies a popular simplification the standard should reject.

## Findings and the standard implications

### 1. Habits: repeated context-linked behaviour, not a 21-day switch

**Evidence — robust.** In a prospective naturalistic study, Lally et al. measured daily repetitions of a chosen healthy behaviour and automaticity. The median time to reach the estimated automaticity plateau was **66 days**, with a very wide **18–254 day** range; a single missed opportunity did not materially derail the trajectory. The result is consistent with habit as learned cue–response association, not a calendar milestone. A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis similarly finds intervention-associated improvement in health-behaviour habit scores, while reporting heterogeneous methods and determinants. [Lally et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674); [Singh et al., 2024 systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39685110/).

**Myth / unsafe generalisation.** “A habit takes 21 days” is not a usable design rule. It is commonly traced to a popular self-help claim, not a general habit-formation trial. Nor is 66 days a deadline, success criterion, or prediction for an individual.

**the standard design implication.** Treat the first **three weeks** as an *early review window*, not habit completion: inspect whether the action was feasible, cueable, and worth keeping. Measure repetitions in a stable context and trend in effort/automaticity only if the person wants it. At a later, explicitly chosen review, decide to continue, redesign, or retire. A missed day creates a recovery prompt, never a reset to “failed.”

### 2. Implementation intentions convert a chosen goal into an executable plan

**Evidence — robust, bounded.** Goal intentions alone show a sizable intention–behaviour gap: experimentally increasing intention produces a smaller subsequent behaviour change (meta-analysis: *d* = 0.36). “If situation X occurs, then I will do Y” implementation intentions improve goal attainment in a meta-analysis of 94 independent tests (*d* = 0.65). [Webb & Sheeran, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16536643/); [Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1).

**Context-dependent.** If–then plans need a real, recognizable cue and an action within the person’s control. They are not a substitute for missing time, access, capability, medical support, or an unresolved underlying goal.

**the standard design implication.** For a voluntary recurring action, offer a compact *plan contract*: `goal/outcome`, `cue or condition`, `next physical action`, `fallback`, and `exit/review date`. Never fabricate the cue or the person’s goal; surface it for confirmation. For example: “After I put my breakfast dish in the sink, I open the task system and select one 10-minute action; if I am away, I do it after the first meal I eat.”

### 3. Environment and cues have leverage, but are not magic

**Evidence — robust mechanism; intervention size is context-dependent.** Habit research identifies stable contextual cues as a core condition for automaticity, and a systematic review finds that context change can create opportunities to alter habitual behaviour (the “habit discontinuity” hypothesis), though studies are heterogeneous. [Gardner, 2015 review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.healthplace.2015.06.008); [Verplanken et al., 2008 natural experiment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvp.2008.04.003).

**the standard design implication.** A failure analysis must examine the physical and digital environment before attributing failure to discipline: distance, preparation, visibility, competing cues, time, access, and social context. The system should record a candidate environmental change as a testable intervention (e.g., charging phone outside bedroom; keeping medication by the coffee machine), its expected mechanism, and the measure—not declare a causal truth.

### 4. Friction, defaults, and choice architecture change behaviour—therefore require consent and reversibility

**Evidence — robust direction; variable magnitude.** A large meta-analysis finds choice-architecture interventions affect behaviour on average, but results differ substantially by technique, domain, and study context. Meta-analysis of default effects likewise finds considerable heterogeneity; defaults work partly through perceived endorsement and the status quo/endowment mechanism. [Mertens et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2107346118); [Jachimowicz et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1017/bpp.2018.13).

**Unsafe generalisation.** “Make the good action frictionless and the bad one hard” can become coercive, inaccessible, or counterproductive. A default is not evidence of the user’s preference; it can exploit inattention.

**the standard design implication.** Defaults are allowed only for reversible, low-stakes personal choices, must be visible and easy to override, and must state the expected benefit and cost. Design `preferred path`, `degraded path`, and `pause/undo path`. Add friction to harmful distractors only when the operator explicitly chooses it and retains an emergency bypass. Measure overrides and burden; frequent override is a design signal, not non-compliance.

### 5. ADHD and variable capacity: design accommodations, not assumptions about a person

**Evidence — robust at the group level.** ADHD is associated with impairments in executive-function-related daily functioning, but people and situations vary substantially. Major clinical guidance includes environmental modifications and supports as components of multi-modal care; it does not justify an app inferring diagnosis or energy from behaviour. [NICE ADHD guideline NG87](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87); [Willcutt et al., 2005 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291704003371).

**Unsupported/unsafe.** “ADHD means a person has no willpower,” “dopamine hacks” reliably solve initiation, or a low output day demonstrates low energy/motivation. Consumer-derived “energy scores” have no general clinical meaning.

**the standard design implication.** Let the user opt into capacity modes and define them in observable terms (e.g., `minimum viable`, `normal`, `high-focus`), each with human-chosen action sizes and degraded paths. Keep an action resumable and provide an explicit “stop safely mid-way” checkpoint. Never diagnose, shame, silently downgrade commitments, or use sensor data to infer capacity without a validated, consented purpose.

### 6. Loss aversion is real enough to design around, not a universal multiplier

**Evidence — robust descriptive phenomenon; heterogeneous magnitude.** Prospect theory introduced reference-dependent evaluation and steeper value for losses than equivalent gains. Subsequent empirical work shows loss aversion varies by task, reference point, and person; it is not a universal 2:1 coefficient. [Kahneman & Tversky, 1979](https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185); [Yechiam, 2019 review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cobeha.2018.08.012).

**the standard design implication.** Make credible opportunity costs and foregone commitments visible before a consequential choice, alongside benefits and uncertainty. Do not frame routine actions as threats or impose artificial loss contracts by default. A decision record should name the reference point used (“what do we risk losing if we wait?”) and separately show the upside, so loss framing cannot dominate unnoticed.

### 7. Quantitative measurement beats retrospective vibes for trend detection; proxies remain fallible

**Evidence — robust.** Retrospective self-report is vulnerable to recall and aggregation errors. Ecological momentary assessment (EMA) samples experiences close to when they occur, reducing retrospective reconstruction; it has its own adherence/reactivity burden. [Shiffman, Stone & Hufford, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.clinpsy.3.022806.091415); [Stone et al., 1998](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1099-1166(199801)13:1%3C45::AID-GPS486%3E3.0.CO;2-M).

**the standard design implication.** Define outcome measures before intervention: one outcome metric, one process metric, a baseline window, review cadence, and a “what would change our mind?” rule. Prefer event logs already produced by normal activity (completion timestamps, transaction records, sleep device data) over burdensome diarying. Show time series and denominators, not only streaks or today’s score. Allow a short optional EMA check-in when interpretation requires context.

### 8. Passive measurement reduces logging burden but is observational, privacy-sensitive, and not diagnosis

**Evidence — promising but immature.** Reviews of smartphone/wearable digital phenotyping find potential for continuous, low-burden behavioural data, but heterogeneity, small samples, scarce external validation, missing data, and privacy problems limit clinical interpretation. [Torous et al., 2024 systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10753422/); [Shrestha et al., 2025 scoping review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12395114/).

**the standard design implication.** Collect passive data only when a named user outcome cannot be measured adequately with a lower-burden, less-sensitive source. Specify source, retention, access, uncertainty, review date, and deletion path. Treat signals as evidence to inspect (“sleep duration fell after change”), never proof of cause, mood, compliance, or diagnosis. Heart-rate changes after diet, for instance, require confounder-aware interpretation and medical escalation where appropriate.

### 9. Lapses should trigger recovery and learning rather than abandonment

**Evidence — robust design direction; exact protocol is context-dependent.** Behaviour maintenance literature distinguishes a lapse (one deviation) from relapse and identifies self-efficacy, coping responses, and environmental triggers as relevant to recovery. Lally et al.’s observation that a single missed repetition did not materially change the habit curve supports avoiding all-or-nothing scoring. [Kwasnicka et al., 2016 systematic review](https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2016.1151372); [Lally et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674).

**the standard design implication.** Every recurring system needs a recoverability contract: it continues safely after silence for at least a month; it preserves the queue and decision context; it chooses the safest degraded path; and resumption begins with a bounded re-entry review, not backlog punishment. On a lapse, log only what is useful: `barrier/cue`, `impact`, `next smallest action`, `environment or system hypothesis`, and whether it needs a design change. Repeated similar lapses open a root-cause investigation.

## Cross-cutting guardrails

1. **Agency before optimization.** Recommendations, defaults, and friction patterns are proposals; high-impact actions remain explicit approvals.
2. **Outcome before proxy.** A metric or streak cannot become the goal. Pair every proxy with the human outcome and a review condition (Goodhart protection).
3. **No hidden interpretations.** Assumptions, uncertainty, data provenance, and model-generated hypotheses are surfaced in human-readable form before action.
4. **Experiment, do not decree.** Changes to the environment or workflow receive a baseline where practical, a timebox, and a stop/revert criterion. Confounding and small samples are stated.
5. **Recovery is first-class.** No response, a lapse, unavailable capability, or low capacity leads to a durable state plus a safe next action—not silent loss or a misleading “complete.”

## Proposed the standard clauses

1. **Habit-review clause:** “The system must not label a behaviour habitual based on elapsed days. Any three-week checkpoint is an early feasibility review; it records repetitions, barriers, cue stability, and whether to continue, redesign, or retire.”
2. **Plan-and-cue clause:** “For a recurring voluntary action, the system may propose an if–then plan only after the outcome, cue, next physical action, fallback, and owner have been surfaced for approval.”
3. **Friction-and-default clause:** “A default or friction intervention must be visible, reversible, proportional to risk, user-authorized, and accompanied by an override/degraded path. Override data is design feedback.”
4. **Measurement clause:** “Before a material intervention, specify the outcome, proxy, baseline, denominator, review date, and disconfirming result. Passive signals are observational evidence with explicit provenance, retention, access, and deletion rules.”
5. **Recovery clause:** “A lapse or absence must preserve state and yield a bounded re-entry path. Repeated barriers create a traceable system-improvement hypothesis; they are not treated as a character judgment.”
