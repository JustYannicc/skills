# Economic design concepts for Thinking in Systems

## Decision

Keep economics as a bounded lens for choosing and checking interventions, not as a universal optimization model. The governing layer includes Satisficing, Opportunity cost, the distinct Pareto principle and Pareto improvement, Marginal analysis, Constraints and the feasible set, evidence-backed Preferences, substitution effects, Cost structure, and Externalities. Numeric optimization and scale analysis remain conditional on explicit objectives, units, evidence, boundaries, and Authority.

## Evidence and source-to-rule inferences

| Concept | Evidence | Appropriate system-design inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Satisficing, marginal analysis, and a stopping rule | Simon's [bounded-rationality account](https://doi.org/10.2307/1884852) supports aspiration-level choice where exhaustive optimization is costly. McCall's [sequential search model](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/84/1/113/1931495) models accepting or rejecting further search under uncertainty. | Keep satisficing as governing vocabulary. State a stopping rule: continue search or preparation only while its expected decision-relevant value exceeds its incremental cost (time, attention, delay, risk, and coordination); otherwise take the named disposition. This is a qualitative decision guard unless the quantities are genuinely measurable. |
| Concentration versus welfare | The [Juran Institute's Pareto account](https://www.juran.com/blog/a-guide-to-the-pareto-principle-80-20-rule-pareto-analysis/) treats 80/20 as a concentration heuristic. Oregon State's [general-equilibrium module](https://open.oregonstate.education/intermediatemicroeconomics/chapter/module-14/) defines a Pareto improvement as making at least one party better off and none worse off. | Retain both as separate governing terms. The **Pareto principle** directs investigation toward potentially high-leverage causes; it neither proves an 80/20 ratio nor permits dropping hard requirements. A **Pareto improvement** is a strict no-material-loser claim for identified affected parties; it does not establish fairness. Never infer one from the other. |
| Constraints, feasible alternatives, preferences, and substitution | OpenStax's [budget-constraint treatment](https://openstax.org/books/principles-microeconomics-2e/pages/2-1-how-individuals-make-choices-based-on-their-budget-constraint) defines an opportunity set and its trade-offs. Its [consumer-choice material](https://openstax.org/books/principles-microeconomics-3e/pages/b-indifference-curves) explains that a price change can induce substitution toward relatively cheaper alternatives. | Keep **Constraints**, the feasible set, evidence-backed Preferences, and substitution effects as governing design material. Changing friction, access, delay, availability, or price can move behavior toward alternatives, so test the actual effect and preserve autonomy. Do not require utility equations or assume stable, complete, individual Preferences. |
| Utility/outcome maximization and expenditure/resource minimization | OpenStax's [consumer-choice summary](https://openstax.org/books/principles-microeconomics-3e/pages/6-1-consumption-choices) derives utility maximization under a consumption budget; it is a specific model with a stated objective and constraint. | Exclude these as governing vocabulary. The universal method chooses an accepted Outcome subject to constraints and burden; it does not claim a single commensurable utility function, a sole decision-maker, or money-minimization. They remain optional local models when an owner has explicitly defined the objective, resource unit, authority, and measurement. |
| Fixed/variable costs, average total cost, and minimum efficient scale | OpenStax [distinguishes fixed, variable, average-total, and marginal cost](https://openstax.org/books/principles-microeconomics-3e/pages/7-3-costs-in-the-short-run). Its [long-run cost discussion](https://openstax.org/books/principles-microeconomics-3e/pages/7-5-costs-in-the-long-run) explains economies of scale. | Keep **Cost structure** as conditional governing vocabulary when recurrence, capacity, or scale can change the decision. A local System may model money, time, attention, support load, or risk as costs if it defines units and avoids double counting. Minimum efficient scale applies only to comparable repeated Outcome units with a credible long-run cost relationship; it is not a universal ideal team, project, or life scale. |
| Externalities | The IMF defines externalities as effects on people not directly involved in a transaction that drive a wedge between private and social costs or returns ([overview](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/fandd/issues/series/back-to-basics/externalities)). Coase's [*The Problem of Social Cost*](https://www.law.uchicago.edu/lawecon/coaseinmemoriam/problemofsocialcost) shows the reciprocal trade-offs and importance of transaction costs. | Keep **Externality** as governing vocabulary for material costs, burdens, risks, or benefits crossing the selected decision boundary without representation in the deciding Actor's or System owner's accountability. This applies to bounded Systems and does not require an economy-wide analysis. |

## Inclusion boundary

**Governing vocabulary/rules:** Satisficing; Opportunity cost; Constraints and the feasible set; Actor Preferences as evidence rather than numerical utility; Pareto principle; Pareto improvement; Marginal analysis and an explicit stopping rule; substitution effects; conditional Cost structure and scale analysis; and Externalities across System boundaries.

**Conditional local analysis:** Outcome maximization under a fixed resource constraint or total-cost minimization subject to the good-enough threshold; fixed, variable, Marginal, and average total cost; and minimum efficient scale. Use them only when a local System has defined units, objective, owner, boundary, evidence, and trade-off Authority.

**Exclude from governing language:** universal utility maximization, economy-wide equilibrium, universal resource or expenditure minimization, a universal cost curve for heterogeneous work, and any claim that scale is inherently efficient. These would turn a cross-domain System-design method into economic planning and obscure rights, Authority, trust, safety, meaning, and irreducible uncertainty.
