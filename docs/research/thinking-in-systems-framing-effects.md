# Framing effects for Thinking in Systems

## Decision

Use framing research as a narrow robustness rule: when materially equivalent options, outcomes, or risks can be expressed in more than one truthful way, compare matched frames to find the frame-robust core of the decision. A choice that changes across those frames is frame-sensitive evidence, not yet evidence of a stable Preference. This supports transparent comparison and a bounded choice; it does **not** authorize a System to steer an Actor through fear, omission, shame, or a frame selected for the designer's proxy.

## Primary-source evidence

| Source | What it establishes | Limit of the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, [2002 Prize press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2002/press-release/) and [Prize summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2002/summary/) | Daniel Kahneman received one half of the 2002 Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel, for integrating psychological research into economics, especially judgment and decision-making under uncertainty. | The recognition is not evidence that every later “nudge,” product pattern, or claim about behavior follows from his work. |
| Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman, [“The Framing of Decisions and the Psychology of Choice” (1981)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7455683), *Science* 211(4481), 453–458 | The authors report predictable preference shifts when equivalent decision problems are formulated differently, including monetary choices and questions about loss of life. In the paper’s hypothetical Asian-disease problem, separate groups received survival or mortality wording for the same expected outcomes and showed opposite aggregate preferences for the certain versus risky program. | The groups each received one wording. The result does not show that every individual reverses, that a particular frame is universally effective, that one framing is objectively correct, or that a choice made after framing reflects an Actor’s enduring Preference. |
| Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky, [“Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk” (1979)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185), *Econometrica* 47(2), 263–291 | The paper models risky choice relative to a reference point, distinguishes gains from losses, and proposes loss aversion as a descriptive feature of its value function. | Reference dependence and loss aversion are related to, but are not synonyms for, every framing effect. The paper is not a mandate to foreground losses. |

## Source-to-rule inference

For a material System interaction, construct two or more matched, truthful formulations of the same feasible alternatives: for example gain and loss, survival and mortality, or cost and avoided cost. Hold outcomes, probabilities, constraints, Authority, alternatives, and material omissions constant; vary only the formulation. If selection is unchanged, that result is stronger evidence for a frame-robust choice under the tested conditions. If selection changes, record a frame-sensitive choice, make the reference point and trade-off explicit, and do not promote either response to a stable Preference without further evidence. Retain a balanced disclosure baseline that shows the material upside, downside, uncertainty, alternatives, and voluntary exit or degraded path; it is not a privileged Decision frame or tie-breaker. Bind any recommendation to the accepted Outcome and the Actor’s Authority; test actual Outcome, burden, and harm rather than treating selection or completion as success.

The 1981 study supports the premise for this check: it demonstrates predictable preference shifts when equivalent decision problems are framed differently. The matched-frame protocol and “frame-robust core” are a System-design inference, not an experimental result claimed by the paper. It does not eliminate genuine ambiguity, change an Actor’s Authority, establish a timeless Preference, or turn a decision into a test the Actor must pass.

This inference fits the governing standard’s existing requirements to inspect **Incentive structure**, Preferences, substitution effects, friction, operating conditions, and proxy-gaming counter-signals. It strengthens its rule to show a credible downside of delay without manufacturing fear, shame, artificial loss or scarcity, streak penalties, or irreversible commitment.

## Do not collapse these concepts

| Concept | Meaning in a System design | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Framing effect** | A decision changes when its presentation or formulation changes, despite a material equivalence claim. | Compare matched truthful frames; do not assume every wording difference is equivalent or causes a predictable response. |
| **Reference dependence / loss framing** | A gain or loss is evaluated relative to a reference point; a loss formulation can be salient. | State the reference point and the real downside only when it is supported. Do not manufacture a loss, scarcity, or penalty. |
| **Defaults / choice architecture** | Arrangement of options, order, presentation, and preselection that affects the path of least resistance. | A default is an incentive-structure and Authority decision. It is not evidence of a framing effect and requires a visible alternative, reversal path, and proportional proof. |
| **Incentives and friction** | Rewards, costs, constraints, information, delay, access, and effort that change the relative attractiveness of actions. | Inspect actual effects and externalized burdens; do not call a friction change “framing” or infer alignment from compliance. |
| **Manipulation** | Using asymmetric information, pressure, concealment, or artificial constraints to obtain the designer’s preferred choice rather than support the Actor’s informed Intent. | Prohibited by the method’s autonomy and bounded-Authority safeguards. Framing evidence describes a vulnerability; it does not justify exploiting it. |

## Claims to avoid

- Do not call the 2002 award a Nobel Prize in the original Nobel categories; use its official name above.
- Do not attribute the framing effect to Kahneman alone: the cited research is by Tversky and Kahneman.
- Do not claim that loss framing reliably motivates every Actor, that it improves welfare or outcomes, or that it should be used to increase completion.
- Do not call agreement across a small set of frames a timeless or complete Preference; it is only stronger evidence for a frame-robust choice under the tested conditions.
- Do not treat a default, nudge, incentive, friction reduction, or choice architecture as framing without showing the relevant mechanism and actual comparison.
- Do not infer consent, stable Preferences, rationality, wellbeing, or Authority from a selected option.
