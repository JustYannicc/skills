Findings

1. Critical — Semantic decision Authority is not required before a canonical
   terminology write.

   Evidence: skills/domain-modeling/SKILL.md:19 checks the record and write
   Authority; line 27 moves from a proposal to an "accepted meaning" without a
   criterion identifying who may accept that meaning; line 35 then authorizes
   persistence solely "within accepted write Authority." Write access to the
   Adapter is not Authority to decide an operative meaning. A record editor can
   therefore canonize a disputed label without an authorized language decision,
   which is an unsafe durable effect.

   Concrete fix: distinguish semantic decision Authority from Adapter write
   Authority in the immediate procedure. Bind the authorized accepting Actor
   and exact accepted proposal revision before changing a canonical meaning;
   where that Authority is absent, preserve the candidates as proposed or
   conflicted and return the smallest decision question. Add a fixture where
   conditional write access exists but semantic decision Authority does not,
   and prove that no canonical write occurs.

2. Critical — The report falsely binds old behavioral runs to the reviewed
   candidate.

   Evidence: evaluations/observations/domain-modeling/direct-nontechnical-1.observation.yaml:5
   claims candidate `working-tree:7e2457...`, while its bound raw evidence at
   evaluations/evidence/domain-modeling/direct-nontechnical-1.txt:9 identifies
   `working-tree:db65a6...` and line 13 identifies runtime SHA-256
   `85ce1f...`; the reviewed runtime SHA-256 is `ff84eae...`. The same mismatch
   affects all three direct technical attempts, and older candidates also
   supply required edge attempts (for example
   evaluations/evidence/domain-modeling/edge-technical-1.txt:9-12). Despite
   that, evaluations/reports/foundation.report.json:36-40 marks the direct
   nontechnical Critical fixture passed at `7e2457...`, and lines 1503-1510
   claim 55/55 passed with no findings. The current revision materially rewrote
   the skill, so rebinding observation YAML cannot substitute for fresh runs.

   Concrete fix: run every required attempt in a fresh context against the
   exact `7e2457...` candidate and `ff84eae...` runtime, preserve those exact
   raw identities, regenerate observations from those runs, and record the
   report. Strengthen deterministic validation so an evidence-declared
   candidate/runtime identity cannot disagree with its observation and report.

3. Major — The model-invocation pointer admits non-terminology boundary and
   relationship work.

   Evidence: skills/domain-modeling/SKILL.md:3 says to use the skill whenever
   "a term, relationship, or boundary is unclear or conflicting." The latter
   two branches can describe architecture, responsibility, policy, dependency,
   or system-design uncertainty even when the language is already operative.
   The body narrows this at line 17, but invocation has already occurred. The
   negative fixtures only cover clear communication and a fix whose terms are
   explicitly defined; neither challenges an unclear architectural or policy
   boundary whose blocker is not terminology.

   Concrete fix: make every description branch explicitly about the operative
   meaning or classification of language, and add technical and nontechnical
   negative fixtures where a relationship/boundary decision is unclear but the
   vocabulary is not. Those cases must stay with their owning capability.

4. Major — Sequential fallback writes can leave one accepted terminology
   change partially committed with no recovery contract.

   Evidence: skills/domain-modeling/SKILL.md:35 permits several entries in one
   accepted change to be written separately when batching is unavailable, but
   lines 35-37 do not require a refreshed guard after each write, define what
   happens if a later write fails, preserve the committed subset, or prevent
   claiming the multi-entry change complete. The only `batchWrite: false`
   fixture, evaluations/fixtures/domain-modeling/edge-technical.fixture.yaml:16,
   also has no write Authority and expects no effect at line 21, so it never
   exercises this fallback.

   Concrete fix: require atomic batch/transaction semantics for mutually
   dependent entries. For genuinely independent sequential entries, refresh
   the exact guard after each commit, stop on failure, return each committed
   identity/result revision plus the uncommitted remainder, and leave the
   aggregate change incomplete with an exact recovery action. Add a partial
   failure fixture for an Adapter without batching.

5. Major — Native-Adapter evidence can be accepted without binding mutable
   source revisions.

   Evidence: skills/domain-modeling/SKILL.md:21 requires sources and evidence
   status but not immutable locators or exact revisions for mutable policy,
   artifact, schema, or observation sources; line 41 likewise returns
   "sources" without revision binding. Exact mutable-source revision is stated
   only in the fallback template at
   skills/domain-modeling/references/language-record-template.md:21, which line
   35 deliberately does not load when an Adapter has a native language shape.
   The exact revision guard at line 37 protects the language record, not the
   evidence used to justify its meaning.

   Concrete fix: put source-bound evidence in the immediate SKILL.md procedure
   for every Adapter shape: every material mutable source needs its locator and
   exact inspected revision, and stale or unverifiable decisive evidence keeps
   the meaning proposed or conflicted. Add a native-Adapter fixture where an
   authoritative source advances between collection and write.

6. Major — Declared degraded and Supplemental branches are not behaviorally
   proven.

   Evidence: skills/domain-modeling/SKILL.md:23 declares both advisory and
   required Supplemental failure behavior, but the only Supplemental fixture,
   evaluations/fixtures/domain-modeling/supplemental-required-stale.fixture.yaml:2-27,
   covers only a required stale result. Line 31 also declares honest behavior
   when the Thinking in Systems relationship authority is unavailable, but no
   Domain Modeling fixture exercises it. Together with the untested no-batch
   success/partial-failure branch above, the passing report does not prove the
   accepted missing-capability, advisory/required, and Adapter capability
   contracts.

   Concrete fix: add fresh-context fixtures for (a) an advisory Supplemental
   failure where core completion remains satisfiable and the failure is
   recorded, (b) an unavailable required Supplemental where durable completion
   blocks, (c) missing Thinking in Systems relationship authority where the
   relationship stays unresolved, and (d) the no-batch partial-write behavior.
   Bind every observation and raw record to the exact reviewed candidate before
   recording a passing report.
