**Governance assessment — community workshop equipment checkout**

**Classification:** Existing repeatable system; its current contract is unknown. Any change could alter access, custody, safety, loss exposure, support burden, and member trust. Treat this as a material change until evidence shows otherwise.

**Outcome:** Members can obtain appropriate equipment safely and fairly, while the workshop can reliably know who has custody, when it is due back, and how exceptions are handled.

**Good-enough threshold:** A proposed change must make normal checkout and return understandable and low-friction, preserve accountability, prevent unauthorized or unsafe use, and leave staff able to resolve disputes, loss, damage, overdue items, and outages without inventing rules.

**Boundary:** Checkout, return, reservation or queueing if used, eligibility, custody records, condition reporting, overdue handling, and recovery are in scope. Broader workshop membership policy, purchasing, and unrelated scheduling remain outside unless they materially constrain these rules.

**Actors and Authority:**

- Members may request, collect, return, and report equipment condition within published eligibility rules.
- Authorized workshop staff determine eligibility exceptions, approve restricted equipment, resolve disputes, and authorize fees or account restrictions.
- The checkout mechanism may record custody and enforce stated rules, but must not invent eligibility, penalties, or policy.
- No LLM should be the sole authority for custody, access, or consequences; deterministic identity, timestamps, inventory state, approvals, and audit records should enforce those boundaries.

**Material unknowns to resolve before changing the system:**

- Which equipment is unrestricted, training-gated, staff-only, consumable, or never loaned?
- What is the authoritative inventory and member-eligibility source?
- What proves handoff, return, condition, and custody transfer?
- Who can override eligibility, extend a due time, waive a consequence, or declare an item lost?
- What currently fails: availability, queue fairness, missing records, returns, damage reporting, staff workload, or something else?
- What privacy, retention, insurance, safety, and accessibility constraints apply?

**Intervention constraint:** Compare doing nothing, clarifying the current process, changing physical/process conditions, configuring an existing checkout solution, adopting a maintained system, and custom work. Include opportunity cost: the best alternative forgone, displaced staff/member attention, switching and maintenance cost, cost of delay, reversibility, and evidence that the option clears the threshold. Do not select an intervention yet.

**Required governing seams:**

- One writable authority for each item’s identity, status, location, condition, and current custodian.
- Explicit handoff contracts for checkout, return, reservation, maintenance hold, and exception handling.
- A visible distinction between available, reserved, checked out, overdue, unavailable, maintenance, missing, and retired states.
- A deterministic effect gate for releasing restricted equipment or recording custody; any LLM use is limited to explanation, triage, or drafting.
- A low-friction normal path that does not make correct use harder than bypassing it.
- Clear treatment of damaged, missing, late, disputed, or incorrectly recorded items.
- A degradation rule for unavailable records or identity verification: preserve state and block unsafe release rather than guessing.
- Recovery rules that reconcile physical inventory with records and preserve history rather than silently rewriting it.

**Proof obligations before a change is accepted:**

- Simulate normal checkout and return, ineligible access, ambiguous item identity, unavailable checkout records, overdue return, damage report, conflicting custody claim, and recovery after reconciliation.
- Verify visible results at the public seams: member instructions, staff authority, custody record, item state, and exception path.
- Validate the real Outcome separately from operational success: reduced checkout friction or loss is not enough if access becomes unfair, unsafe, confusing, or burdensome.
- Measure burden and harm alongside the Outcome, including staff exception work, member confusion, accessibility barriers, disputes, and workarounds.

**Legacy and change constraint:** Any selected change needs an explicit disposition for existing loans, reservations, condition history, member permissions, and items whose status is unknown. Historical records remain historical; do not rewrite them to imply the new rules always applied.

**Permitted next work:** Preserve the current process and gather the smallest evidence that resolves the material unknowns above. Hand this governing assessment to `'Workflow'`; `'Workflow'` may decide phases and coordination, but no change should be treated as complete until its contract, authority, recovery path, and proof are explicit.
