This is a material change to the intake-classification System, not an instruction update. It changes the rule for future intake and reinterprets existing cases, so it requires a successor System Record and a linked Change and Legacy Record before Workflow coordinates implementation.

## Governing constraints

- **Outcome:** every case is classified by one accepted rule without losing the fact that older cases were created under the predecessor. Good enough is a simulated, bounded transition with every discoverable case accounted for or explicitly reported as unknown, a safe rollback or repair path, and no live rule change yet.
- **Boundary:** include the intake rule, new and existing cases, the people and systems that create, classify, use, support, audit, or recover cases, and any reports, queues, automations, or downstream decisions that consume the classification. Reopen the boundary for every newly affected actor, interface, permission, state transition, exception, or support duty. The surrounding organization retains ownership of the integrated outcome; Workflow retains parent responsibility and phase coordination.
- **Authority:** an agent may inventory, draft, classify, simulate, and review. It may not apply the rule, migrate cases, expand authority, approve the successor contract, or activate it without an approval bound to the exact proposed revision and its assumptions. The responsible business owner must approve the successor rule, cohort treatment, rollout, and rollback conditions.
- **Facts and gaps:** preserve the exact old rule, proposed successor rule, original case classifications, provenance, and decision history. Name unresolved scope—especially cases that cannot be discovered, read, or deterministically reclassified—instead of treating them as migrated.

## Proposed transition contract

1. Publish a proposed successor System Record: rule version and applicability; authoritative writable classification state; actors and handoffs; dependent interfaces; approval and effect gates; and the Normal, Degraded, Paused, Recovery, reset, and retirement rules.
2. Create a Change and Legacy Record. Discover the affected population, preserve the original capture and prior classification, and classify each discoverable cohort as `unaffected`, `compatible`, `auto-adapt`, `review-required`, `conflict`, `exempt`, or `unreachable/unknown`. Each cohort needs an exact transformation or re-evaluation rule, owner, and completion evidence.
3. Simulate the production-intended transformation with all live effectors disabled. Render proposed writes, queue or report changes, exceptions, legacy counts, and rollback steps. Cover normal cases, ambiguous cases, missing inputs, unavailable dependencies, incorrect assumptions, degraded operation, recovery, and unknown legacy coverage. Correct rejected material cases and replay them before approval.
4. Use a bounded, resumable rollout only after approval. Keep an immutable predecessor reference or history, monitor classification correctness and burden on affected actors, and make rollback or repair available until the agreed verification gate passes. Retire the predecessor only after intake has stopped using it, outstanding legacy work has a disposition, required history remains accessible, and dependent systems have been notified.

## Operating conditions and incentives

The intended path must be easier than bypassing or silently relabeling cases. Inspect whether speed targets, dashboards, queue pressure, permissions, training, missing context, or downstream incentives reward misclassification or shift review burden onto staff. For any migration metric, state the Outcome it represents, how it could improve while case quality worsens, and a counter-signal—for example, compare migration coverage with sampled decision correctness, exception volume, and reviewer burden.

- **Normal:** apply the approved successor rule to eligible cases and retain predecessor provenance.
- **Degraded:** permit only pre-authorized classification or safe queueing; expose missing inputs or dependency limits and block downstream effects that require a reliable classification.
- **Paused:** preserve case state and prevent reclassification when authority, required input, simulation evidence, or a critical dependency is missing; show the unblock condition.
- **Recovery:** restore the accepted baseline from preserved state, reconcile attempted changes against the simulation/ledger, verify the affected cohort, then resume in bounded steps. A recurring failure requires correcting its governing cause and replaying the failure class before restoring the same autonomy.

## Proof and decision gates

Verification is that the approved rule, transformations, interfaces, and recovery behavior operate as contracted. Validation is that classifications support the intended real decisions. Track burden and harm separately, including review load, delays, confusion, privacy exposure, and wrongly affected cases. Unknown or unreachable legacy coverage remains a visible cohort and blocks any claim of complete migration.

The least-cost next action is exploration: have Workflow obtain the authoritative old and proposed rules, owners and approvers, case inventory/access limits, dependent-system contracts, and the decision the new classification governs; then draft the two records and run the zero-effect simulation. Do not make a live rule change or declare the Outcome complete.
