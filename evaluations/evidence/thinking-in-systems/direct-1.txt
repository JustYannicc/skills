**Assessment: community workshop equipment checkout**

**Interpretation and boundary**

Assess the existing repeatable checkout system before changing it. The intended Outcome is that authorized participants can obtain and return equipment safely, fairly, and with enough traceability to resolve loss, damage, overdue items, and disputes.

This assessment governs the system’s constraints; it does not select `'Workflow'` phases or plan the change.

**Material unknowns to resolve**

- Who may borrow, approve exceptions, administer inventory, impose restrictions, and decide repairs or replacement?
- What equipment, users, locations, storage states, and handoffs are in scope?
- What currently happens at checkout, return, overdue, damage, loss, reservation conflict, staff absence, or record disagreement?
- Which existing record is authoritative for item identity, availability, borrower responsibility, condition, and history?
- What outcome threshold matters: fewer missing items, faster access, safer use, lower staff burden, clearer accountability, or another priority?
- What evidence currently shows the problem and its scale?

Do not treat a preferred tool, sign-out form, lock, or application as the Outcome until these are answered.

**Governing constraints**

- Assign explicit Authority. A volunteer, staff member, participant, or LLM may propose a checkout decision but cannot create borrowing, disciplinary, purchasing, privacy, or policy authority by implication.
- Give each material fact one writable authority: item identity, current custody, condition, eligibility, reservation, approval, and event history. Dashboards, labels, spreadsheets, and generated views must identify their source and freshness rather than become competing editable records.
- Define each handoff: borrower → checkout steward → equipment → return inspection → inventory record. Each needs inputs, completion evidence, exception handling, and recovery behavior.
- Separate LLM judgment from deterministic enforcement. An LLM can interpret free-text condition notes or propose communications; stable identity, availability, eligibility rules, approvals, timestamps, custody transitions, and effect gates need deterministic enforcement.
- Inspect incentives and friction for every actor. The safe, accurate return path should be easier than bypassing it; avoid shame, hidden pressure, or metrics that reward transaction volume while loss, safety, or access worsens.
- Include real operating conditions: unattended periods, missing approvers, damaged or unidentifiable items, conflicting records, interrupted checkout, unavailable storage or connectivity, and disputes. Define Normal, Degraded, Paused, and Recovery behavior before relying on the system.
- Preserve historical facts. A correction or migration must show what changed; it must not rewrite prior custody, condition, or policy history as though the new rule always applied.

**Intervention decision rule**

Compare, rather than assume, these options:

1. Preserve the current process.
2. Wait for specific baseline evidence.
3. Change the physical/process environment—for example, storage access, item labels, return location, or inspection timing.
4. Configure an existing maintained checkout system.
5. Build a custom system.

Choose the least costly reversible option that clears the agreed threshold after considering opportunity cost, displaced staff/participant time, transition cost, ongoing ownership, delay, reversibility, and evidence value. A bounded observation or simulation is preferable to committing while the baseline or Authority is unclear.

**Proof required before a consequential change**

The proposed system should be able to show, at its public seams:

- an eligible borrower checks out a known item;
- ineligible, ambiguous, or conflicting requests are safely handled;
- return and condition evidence update the authoritative record;
- unavailable staff or dependencies produce a visible degraded or paused path without untracked custody;
- an error, lost item, or disputed record can be reconciled and recovered;
- existing inventory and outstanding loans receive an explicit transition disposition;
- the change improves the intended Outcome without disproportionate burden, confusion, privacy loss, or exclusion.

**Permitted next work**

Preserve the current process description and collect the smallest baseline needed to answer the unresolved questions. No policy, Authority, data migration, external effect, or durable tool choice should be treated as approved until the responsible workshop authority accepts the Outcome, boundary, and decision threshold.
