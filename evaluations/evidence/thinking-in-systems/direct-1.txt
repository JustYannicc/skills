## Governance assessment: community workshop equipment checkout

**Accepted interpretation.** This is assessment of a recurring, human-and-physical-system process before any change is planned. The intended outcome is that members can obtain and return equipment reliably, safely, and fairly, while the workshop retains accurate custody and condition information. “Good enough” is not merely a faster checkout: normal use must have a clear accountable handoff, equipment must be findable and returnable, and exceptions must be visible and recoverable without relying on memory or blame.

**Boundary and authoritative state.** Workflow should establish the supported users, equipment classes, locations, opening/access conditions, and handoffs (request, authorization, issue, return, inspection, maintenance). It must name the authoritative record for each item’s identity, availability, current custodian, due/return status, condition, restrictions, and incident history; a sign-out sheet or tool view cannot become a competing editable truth. Preserve original requests, existing records, and corrections with provenance. Changes affecting access rights, fees, safety rules, or inventory disposition require the workshop’s designated authority; the process may not infer that authority.

**Material seams to govern.**

- Actors and authority: member, checkout volunteer/staff, maintenance/safety owner, and process owner need explicit permissions for reservation, release, override, condition assessment, and escalation. Each handoff needs a human-readable contract: required input, output, timing, authority, failure behavior, and completion evidence.
- Incentives and friction: make the compliant path (locate item, understand eligibility, record custody, return it) easier than workarounds. Inspect incentives for unrecorded borrowing, delayed returns, over-reservation, or punitive enforcement. Use proportionate, reversible, user-authored friction; do not use shame, hidden penalties, or proxy metrics as the outcome.
- Environment: inspect storage layout, labels, visibility, distance to the checkout point, required accessories, opening-time access, queueing, return location, inspection space, cues, and competing routes before proposing software or additional rules. Treat changes as reversible hypotheses with a mechanism, baseline where practical, success/abort rule, and reversal path.
- Proof: define observable evidence at the public seams—for example, an item can be located; its recorded custodian and condition match reality; a return/inspection is acknowledged; and an exception is discoverable. Separate evidence that the process ran from evidence that access, safety, fairness, and equipment availability actually improved, and track burden such as waiting, search time, confusion, and volunteer load.

**Lowest-common-denominator and recovery contract.** The design must state what is never lost (custody, safety/condition facts, and unresolved exceptions), the smallest valid action when a member, volunteer, network, or record system is unavailable, and what may safely continue without human attention. In degraded operation, preserve the original request and item state, allow only pre-authorized safe actions, and keep blocked checkout/return cases visible. In paused operation, prevent unrecorded release or other unauthorized effects. Recovery should reconcile physical inventory with the authoritative record, triage the bounded exception set, resolve stale or disputed custody/condition facts, restore the agreed baseline, and show verification—without an unbounded backlog or requiring people to reconstruct context from memory.

**Open governance gaps for Workflow to resolve before planning a material change.** The assessment lacks the workshop’s current process and baseline, safety/legal constraints, item risk classes, designated decision and override authorities, existing source of truth, access/identity model, acceptable delay/burden, and incident/escalation rules. These are material because different answers change the authority envelope, support boundary, degraded path, and proof threshold. Until they are resolved, preserve current evidence and limit work to discovery or reversible observation; do not authorize a new process, durable record, or external effect.

**Applicable governing clauses and proof obligations.** Sections 1–3 require the outcome, boundary, authority, evidence, and failure-state check; section 4 governs least-cost/reversible intervention, incentives, environment, authority, source preservation, handoffs, and degradation/recovery; section 6 requires named public proof seams and distinguishes design from live operation; section 9 requires normal, degraded, paused, and recovery behavior. If Workflow proposes a material process change, it must also preserve the original process/state, define its support matrix and authoritative record, test the intended path without live effects, and obtain the required approval before activation.

**Workflow boundary.** This assessment supplies governance constraints and gaps only. Workflow retains parent responsibility for deciding whether and how to plan, gathering missing facts, and producing any phase results; no specialist phase has been selected or executed.

**State:** ready for Workflow decision.
