A feedback loop is a cycle in which a system’s output becomes information that influences its next actions: the system observes a result, compares it with a goal or condition, and adjusts (or reinforces) its behavior. In a negative feedback loop, the adjustment counteracts a deviation—like a thermostat turning heat on when a room is too cold—so the system tends toward stability; in a positive feedback loop, the adjustment amplifies a change—like microphone feedback getting louder—so the system can accelerate, grow, or become unstable.
A feedback loop is a cycle in which a system’s results feed back into the system and influence what happens next. In a reinforcing loop, an effect amplifies itself—for example, more word-of-mouth can bring more customers, creating still more word-of-mouth. In a balancing loop, the feedback counteracts change—for example, a thermostat turns the heat down as a room warms, helping keep the temperature near its setting.
