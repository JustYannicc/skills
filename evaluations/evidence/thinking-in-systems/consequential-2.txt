This is a material, durable rule change—not a routine reclassification. It affects every new case and reinterprets existing cases created under a prior contract, so the system boundary is reopened. Do not apply the new rule next week yet.

## Governing assessment for Workflow

- **Outcome to bind:** the intended improvement from the new classification rule, its non-outcomes, success threshold, and why changing existing cases is necessary. “Use the new rule everywhere” is a proposed means, not yet an accepted outcome.
- **Authority:** identify who may approve the successor rule, its scope, any authority expansion, and the exact cutover/rollback decision. Existing approvals, estimates, and proof do not automatically transfer to the new rule or legacy population.
- **Authoritative state and originals:** retain every original intake, its former classification, governing-rule version, provenance, and any user edits. The migration may add a new classification/disposition; it must not rewrite history as though the new rule always governed.
- **Boundary and seams:** document affected actors, intake sources, downstream consumers, interfaces, permissions, handoffs, queues, reports, automation, exceptions, support ownership, and environmental/incentive effects. In particular, inspect what the rule makes easier to classify, avoid, game, or silently misroute, and the friction/correction route for people affected by a changed classification.
- **Evidence and unknowns:** state the evidence for the new rule, material assumptions, competing adequate alternatives, and a discoverable coverage query for existing cases. Unknown, inaccessible, malformed, or ambiguous legacy cases remain explicitly counted and visible; they cannot be silently treated as migrated.

## Required change and legacy contract

Before adoption, create a proposed successor System Record and a linked, versioned Change and Legacy Record. The latter must define the current/proposed rule versions; approval; affected populations and information types; coverage query; per-case disposition (`unaffected`, `compatible`, `auto-adapt`, `review-required`, `conflict`, `exempt`, or `unreachable/unknown`); exact proposed transformation or re-evaluation; cohort sequencing; verification counts; rollback/repair; monitoring; cutover; and predecessor retirement.

Proposed legacy disposition: discover and classify every reachable existing case by the legacy dispositions above. Auto-adapt only cases whose required inputs, confidence, and downstream effects satisfy the approved deterministic rule. Route ambiguous, conflicting, high-consequence, or missing-evidence cases to `review-required` or `conflict`; preserve `unreachable/unknown` as an open coverage gap. New intake remains on the current approved rule until an approved cutover rule says otherwise.

## Operating safeguards

Define these modes before Workflow coordinates the change:

- **Normal:** process cases under the currently approved version; expose version and classification evidence.
- **Degraded:** when a dependency, source, or classifier is unavailable, preserve intake and use only the pre-authorized lowest-effort safe path; do not guess a consequential classification.
- **Paused:** preserve state, stop reclassification and downstream effects where no safe useful action remains, and make the blocker and owner visible.
- **Recovery:** reconcile partial cohorts from preserved state, resolve stale assumptions and failures, restore the approved baseline, verify counts and downstream consistency, then resume bounded work without backlog punishment.

Workflow must require zero-effect simulation before any live change: use the production-intended retrieval, interpretation, decision, and composition path with all production effectors and durable/user-visible writes disabled. Simulate representative new, legacy, ambiguous, unavailable-dependency, low-capacity, recovery, and rollback cases; render exact proposed classifications and downstream effects; prove that the live path differs only at the named effector boundary. Rejected cases require correction and replay.

Approval may then authorize a bounded rollout/pilot, with resumable application, monitoring for outcome, fidelity/burden, and unintended incentives, plus a tested rollback/repair path. A live rule change, bulk reclassification, or claim of completion is outside this assessment. Workflow retains parent responsibility for phase coordination and the final decision.
