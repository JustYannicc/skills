**Governing assessment — community workshop equipment checkout**

**Accepted interpretation.** Assess the current and proposed equipment-checkout system before changing it. The intended Outcome is that authorized people can obtain suitable equipment safely, each item’s custody and condition are knowable, returns happen reliably, and the process remains workable for members and workshop operators.

**Boundary and Authority.** This covers request, approval, checkout, custody, return, condition reporting, overdue/lost/damaged handling, inventory state, and operator handoffs. It does not authorize changing policy, purchasing equipment, imposing penalties, collecting new personal data, or activating automation. Those require an explicitly authorized decision.

**Material unknowns to resolve before a consequential change.**

- Who may check out which equipment, under what training or supervision conditions?
- Which items require reservation, inspection, consumables, deposits, or operator approval?
- What is the authoritative inventory and custody record today?
- What counts as a successful return: physical location, condition check, cleaning, accessory completeness, and record update?
- Who can resolve disputes, override a block, mark damage, or write off a missing item?
- What operating conditions matter: unattended periods, busy events, unavailable operators, poor connectivity, inaccessible storage, or interrupted returns?
- What existing loans, reservations, and informal records would a new rule affect?

**Intervention constraint.** Compare doing nothing, clarifying the existing process, changing the physical/process environment, configuring an existing maintained tool, and custom building. Prefer the least costly reversible option that can meet the threshold. Include opportunity cost: staff time, member friction, displaced workshop activity, ongoing maintenance, and the cost of delay. Waiting is acceptable only with a named evidence trigger and recheck condition.

**System seams that need a governing rule.**

- **Actors and handoffs:** member, approving operator, inventory custodian, maintenance/support owner, and any system. Each handoff needs input, output, timing, Authority, failure behavior, and visible completion evidence.
- **State:** preserve original request, approval, item identity, borrower/custodian, checkout time, due condition, return evidence, condition history, and any override. One writable authority must own each material state; a dashboard or generated list cannot silently become a second authority.
- **LLM and enforcement:** an LLM may help interpret a request or draft a notice. Deterministic mechanisms must enforce identity, eligibility, availability, approvals, state transitions, due-time rules, and effect gates.
- **Incentives and friction:** make the safe, correct path easier than informal borrowing; do not substitute shame, hidden surveillance, or proxy metrics for the Outcome.
- **Operating modes:** define Normal, Degraded, Paused, and Recovery behavior. For example, if the record system is unavailable, define exactly what may be loaned, what paper or temporary state is preserved, who reconciles it, and what proves recovery.
- **Legacy and change:** any new rules or record must classify current loans, reservations, inventory discrepancies, and historical damage rather than overwriting history.

**Good-enough proof.** A proposed change should demonstrate visible outcomes for: normal checkout and return; unavailable or already-loaned equipment; missing approval/training; damaged or incomplete return; unavailable record system; interrupted checkout; overdue item; reconciliation after degradation; and affected existing loans. Verify that the rules execute as intended, validate that custody and access improve, and measure burden separately for members and operators.

**Persistence.** This is a durable system, so any approved design needs one canonical System Record with its Authority, supported conditions, state ownership, operating modes, proof, and change/legacy treatment. No change is authorized by this assessment alone.

`'Workflow'` may use these constraints to coordinate the next work, but this assessment does not select phases or claim the parent outcome is complete.
