## Governance assessment: community workshop equipment checkout

**Route and boundary.** This is a governance assessment for a potentially durable, safety- and access-relevant process; it authorizes no process change. The system of interest is the checkout-to-return lifecycle for workshop equipment. Its containing system is the community workshop's operations and safety program. Likely subsystems are inventory/identity records, eligibility and training verification, checkout and return handoffs, condition maintenance, and incident handling. Upstream systems may include membership, training, access control, maintenance, insurance, and applicable safety rules; dependent systems include members' work, staff/support, and equipment availability. The process boundary and every dependency must be confirmed before a proposed change binds them.

**Intent, Outcome, and threshold.** Preserve the request as an assessment before planning. The likely Intent is shared access to equipment without avoidable injury, loss, conflict, or unaccountable burden. A proposed Outcome must make equipment availability, authorized custody, condition, and return status visible enough for members and operators to act safely. A good-enough change must: (1) prevent or hold for review any checkout that lacks required eligibility or Authority; (2) retain a single accountable custody and condition record; (3) make returns, overdue items, damage, and incidents recoverable; and (4) show that safety, access, and support burdens have not worsened materially. These are proposed constraints until the workshop's authorized owner confirms them.

**Authority and ownership.** The workshop's designated operator or governing body must own the integrated Outcome, approve policy and any external effect, and name responsible actors for eligibility, custody, maintenance, support, and recovery. A member may request, receive, and return equipment only within the published eligibility and checkout contract. Staff or volunteers may perform assigned checks, but cannot infer a new exception or policy from convenience. The Workflow skill retains parent responsibility, phase selection, coordination, and terminal proof; this assessment supplies constraints only.

**Material seams and rules to establish.**

- Each equipment item needs a stable identity; the custody/condition record needs one writable authority, an auditable history, and a visible freshness or failure state. Derived lists must identify their source and revision.
- Every handoff needs a producer, consumer, preconditions, expected output, completion evidence, timing expectation, effect boundary, and degraded/retry/resumption behavior. At minimum: member request -> eligibility decision -> custody assignment -> return/inspection -> availability or maintenance hold.
- Define eligibility evidence, who may override it, whether any item requires training or supervision, and how a denied or ambiguous request is handled. No matching eligible rule enters an unknown-case path rather than creating an implicit exception.
- Define condition vocabulary and inspection evidence at checkout and return; preserve original incident reports and condition history rather than overwriting them.
- Inspect actual incentives and friction: members may avoid a slow or embarrassing checkout, volunteers may optimize queue speed, and operators may lack time to reconcile records. The intended safe path must be easier than unofficial borrowing, using clear defaults, prepared choices, and exception-based attention rather than shame or artificial scarcity.
- Inspect operating conditions that alter behavior: busy periods, volunteer absence, poor connectivity, physical layout, inaccessible storage, urgent project needs, and competing access paths. Any environmental intervention needs a mechanism, proof seam, abort rule, and reversal path.
- Identify privacy, retention, and access rules for member identity, training, custody, and incident data before collecting or exposing them.

**Intervention decision constraints.** Workflow should compare doing nothing, waiting for named facts, changing the physical/operating process, configuring an existing maintained system, adopting a maintained solution, and building custom capability. Use concentration evidence to test which failures cause most loss, delay, or unsafe access; retain safeguards for rare catastrophic cases, rights, accessibility, trust, recovery, and explicit duties. Compare the best forgone alternative, displaced volunteer time and attention, transition and ownership costs, delay cost, reversibility, and burdens shifted to members, maintainers, or future operators. Do not treat sunk effort or a convenience metric as justification. Further discovery is worthwhile only where an answer could change the feasible set or selected intervention; the smallest current discriminating questions are: who has Authority for policy exceptions, what eligibility and safety requirements apply by item class, and what baseline evidence exists for losses, damage, queue burden, and unofficial borrowing.

**Modes, degradation, and recovery.** The proposed contract must define Normal, Degraded, Paused, and Recovery modes. In a degraded condition such as an unavailable record, absent approver, or uncertain eligibility, allow only pre-authorized actions that preserve state or safely limit harm; block unverified checkout and make the limitation visible. When no safe useful action remains, pause effects while preserving the request and unblock condition. Recovery must reconcile custody and condition against preserved evidence, triage missing or damaged items in bounded batches, verify the healthy baseline, and resume only after the responsible owner accepts the result. Define triggers, allowed and blocked effects, preserved state, visibility, owner, retry/resumption rule, and exit proof for each mode.

**Proof and learning obligations.** Before any live change, simulate the intended operational path without live writes or effects. Cover normal checkout/return, ambiguous eligibility, missing or unavailable records, overload or reduced staffing, damage or overdue return, recovery, and affected existing items. The proof seam must show the exact proposed custody, eligibility, condition, notices, and blocked effects. Measure separately: contract adherence, the real access-and-safety Outcome, member/volunteer burden, and sustainment. Each metric needs an underlying Outcome, gaming route, counter-signal, decision, and retirement condition; for example, faster checkout may conceal less verification, while low incident reports may conceal under-reporting. A material change needs a successor steady-state contract plus a change-and-legacy record that classifies existing equipment, current loans, training records, and unresolved incidents; defines bounded, resumable rollout, rollback/repair, verification, and predecessor disposition.

**State returned to Workflow.** Ready for a Workflow decision once the named authority, boundary, baseline, item-class requirements, and existing-state cohorts are supplied. No Workflow phase has been selected, no plan or specialist result has been created, and no process effect has occurred.
