## Governance assessment — community workshop equipment checkout

**Accepted interpretation.** The request is to govern Workflow’s planning of a possible change to an existing, recurring equipment-checkout process—not to select or execute a change. The intended outcome is a checkout process that gives authorized workshop members dependable access to equipment while preserving safety, accountability, and the ability to return equipment to known available condition. A sufficient result for this assessment is a bounded set of constraints and the unanswered decisions Workflow must resolve before proposing a change.

This is a potential material change: it may alter rules, authority, records, handoffs, access, physical environment, and recovery duties. No change, new authority, or external effect is approved by this assessment. Workflow retains parent responsibility for deciding which planning or specialist phases, if any, to run.

### Governing constraints for Workflow

- **Preserve the original process and source facts.** Record the current checkout and return path, existing rules, incidents, and any member corrections without overwriting them. Separate observed facts from assumptions. (Standard §§1–3, §4.6–7.)
- **Frame the outcome before choosing a tool or intervention.** Define the present failure or opportunity, the desired human outcome, good-enough threshold, non-goals, support boundary, and the smallest adequate alternative. Compare no change, an environmental/process change, configuration or an existing maintained solution, and custom work if that becomes relevant. (Standard §§3–4, §12.)
- **Make authority explicit.** Identify who may check equipment out, approve exceptions, change inventory or availability, resolve disputes/damage, and authorize any purchase, policy, access-control, or notification effect. Model confidence or convenience does not create authority. (Standard §3.7; §4.5, §4.9.)
- **Name authoritative state and each handoff.** For equipment identity, availability/location, custody, condition, reservations, maintenance/lockout status, and incident history, name one writable authority and human-readable visibility. Each member-to-system, member-to-steward, and checkout-to-return handoff needs its input, output, timing, authority, failure behavior, and visible completion proof. (Standard §4.9, §4.15; §6.)
- **Inspect incentives, friction, and the physical environment.** Determine what makes returning, reporting damage, and checking availability easy or costly; what makes bypassing the process rewarding or invisible; and how layout, labels, storage location, keys/devices, queues, opening hours, social context, and competing cues affect behavior. Use visible, user-authored, reversible friction for harmful paths; do not use shame, hidden manipulation, or proxy metrics as the outcome. (Standard §4.3–4; §9.)
- **Design for the lowest-capacity real condition.** The eventual proposal must state the smallest valid checkout/return action when a member is rushed, a steward is unavailable, connectivity or the record system is down, information is stale, or no one responds for an extended period. It must preserve essential state, block unsafe or unauthorized release, keep the blocked reason visible, prevent unbounded queues or reminders, and give a bounded recovery path without reconstructing context or punishing backlog. (Standard §4.2, §4.12; §9.)
- **Treat safety and condition exceptions as normal modes.** The later contract must define normal, degraded, paused, and recovery modes; triggers; allowed and forbidden actions; owner; preserved information; re-entry proof; and how equipment suspected damaged, missing, or unsafe is made unavailable pending authorized resolution. (Standard §9; §6.)
- **Define proof before implementation.** Workflow should require observable, human-visible acceptance cases: for example, a normal checkout and return, unavailable or damaged equipment, a missing/stale record, an unauthorized request, and a no-steward/no-connection case. If a change is proposed, test its real decision/composition path with effectors and durable/user-visible writes disabled before any live pilot; an approved bounded pilot supplies validation. Design status must remain separate from implementation or live status. (Standard §4.10–11; §6.)
- **Measure outcome, operation, and burden separately.** A later proposal needs a baseline and decision rule that distinguish dependable/safe availability from process compliance and from burden such as member effort, confusion, pressure, wait time, steward workload, privacy cost, or displaced work. A high checkout count or low reported-damage count alone is not success. (Standard §4.11; §6; §10.)
- **Reopen the boundary for material scope expansion.** Adding user groups, equipment classes, rooms, access devices, payment/deposit rules, integrations, permissions, states, exceptions, or recovery obligations requires a fresh boundary and legacy assessment. Prior evidence, estimates, authority, and approval do not automatically carry over. A material accepted change ultimately needs a proposed successor System Record and linked Change and Legacy Record before adoption. (Standard §4.14; §12.)

### Open decision gaps for Workflow

Before a change can be framed, establish at least: the current process and observed failure modes; the equipment and member support matrix; safety, insurance, and privacy constraints; the current authoritative records; accountable roles and approval limits; the realistic lowest-capacity/environmental conditions; and the baseline evidence and acceptable burden.

**State:** ready for Workflow decision. Workflow may now choose proportionate discovery and planning work, or pause for the smallest needed facts. This assessment supplies governance only; it does not select phases, create phase results, or implement a solution.
