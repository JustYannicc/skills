## Governing assessment — proposed intake-classification change

This is a material change to a Durable system, not a rule edit. It applies to future intake and to cases created under the current rule, so it changes stored state, classifications, operating behavior, recovery obligations, and the contract relied on by people and dependent systems. Reopen the system boundary before Workflow coordinates the change.

### Outcome, boundary, and Authority

- **Intent / Outcome:** have each applicable case classified under an accepted successor rule without losing the original record, creating an untraceable mixed population, or interrupting safe intake.
- **Good-enough threshold:** a successor rule, migration treatment, and operating controls are accepted only when every discoverable legacy case has a visible disposition; unknown coverage remains explicit; simulation shows the exact proposed changes without effects; and rollback or repair is possible.
- **Boundary:** includes the intake rule, new-case flow, existing case records and their history, classification consumers, human operators, support/recovery, and the next-week cutover. It excludes changing any external dependent system without its owner’s agreement.
- **Authority gap:** approval is required from the owner with authority over the successor rule, legacy-case treatment, any external effects, and activation. The requested assessment authorizes neither changing classifications nor committing a cutover.

### Required successor and change controls

Workflow should obtain a proposed successor System Record and linked Change and Legacy Record. They must name the System owner; each actor’s responsibility; the authoritative source for current and historical classifications; the rule version and effective time; inputs, outputs, handoffs, and dependent consumers; and the deterministic migration, approval, version-binding, reconciliation, and rollback guards. Historical captures and prior classifications remain intact; the successor classification is a versioned result, not a rewrite of history.

Assess actual incentives and friction before adoption: for example, a rule that improves intake speed while shifting ambiguous cases, correction work, or support burden to another team is a trade-off, not a Pareto improvement. Name the affected actor, the authority that may accept that burden, its mitigation, and a counter-signal for any volume or speed metric.

### Legacy disposition and simulation

Discover the legacy population from the authoritative case source, preserve the population snapshot and rule versions, and classify each discoverable case as `unaffected`, `compatible`, `auto-adapt`, `review-required`, `conflict`, `exempt`, or `unreachable/unknown`. For each cohort, record the exact transformation or re-evaluation, responsible actor, completion evidence, and any required approval. Report coverage counts and unknown coverage; do not infer that unobserved cases are compatible.

Run a zero-effect simulation through the production-intended retrieval, interpretation, decision, and composition path. It must render exact proposed classifications, writes, notices, unresolved ambiguities, and rollback/repair actions while disabling live effectors and durable/user-visible writes. Representative cases include normal intake, ambiguous cases, missing inputs, unavailable dependencies, stale rule assumptions, every affected legacy cohort, partial migration interruption, and recovery after a rejected transformation. Correct and replay every rejected material case before activation is considered.

### Operating and recovery contract

| Mode | Trigger and allowed behavior | Proof to exit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | Accepted successor version is bound; new cases use it and legacy work proceeds only under its approved cohort treatment. | Reconciliation confirms rule-version binding and cohort evidence. |
| Degraded | Missing dependency, ambiguity, or capacity constraint: preserve the case and original classification; allow only pre-authorized safe intake or queueing; block irreversible reclassification and unapproved notices. | Limitation is visible, dependency/assumption is restored, and affected cases are reconciled. |
| Paused | No safe classification or effect can be made. Preserve the exact request, source state, proposed revision, and unblock condition; prevent cutover and migration effects. | Required authority, capability, or evidence is restored and the proposal is revalidated. |
| Recovery | A failed, partial, or incorrect migration: stop further affected effects, use preserved snapshots and event history to triage the bounded cohort, repair or roll back, then replay simulation and reconciliation. | The accepted baseline is restored or the successor is explicitly revised, with affected-case evidence. |

Define queue ownership, retry/resumption behavior, stale-assumption handling, change notifications to dependent systems, and a visible control surface for exceptions. The LLM may identify ambiguity or compose proposals; deterministic mechanisms must own rule-version binding, durable state, transformations, approvals, retries, and reconciliation.

### Evidence, decision, and next step

Verification is that the accepted rule and cohort transformations operate exactly as specified. Validation is that the intended classification outcome improves in real conditions. Track burden separately: correction rate, review queue age, support load, displaced work, and harmful misclassification, alongside an outcome signal and a counter-signal for any proxy. Define the observation window from the expected effect of the rule and the decision it will inform.

Before activation, Workflow should coordinate the required decision on the exact successor rule, legacy treatments, rollout/cutover scope, rollback or repair path, and authority. Workflow retains parent responsibility and terminal proof. No live rule change, migration, or claim of completion has occurred.
