## Governance assessment — proposed intake-classification change

**Accepted interpretation:** This is a material, consequential change: it replaces the classification rule for both future intake and existing cases. It reopens the system boundary; it is not an authorized live rule change. Workflow retains parent responsibility for coordinating the decision and any later transition.

**Outcome and boundary:** The proposed outcome is a consistently governed intake population under a successor rule, without losing original captures, historical classifications, provenance, or the ability to explain each case's transition. The change boundary includes the authoritative rule and version, all new cases after cutover, the discoverable pre-existing population, dependent interfaces and reports, classification-state transitions, exceptions, permissions, support/recovery duties, and retirement of the predecessor rule. The exact successor rule, authoritative owner, affected-system inventory, legacy coverage, and approval are not supplied; they remain visible gaps and block adoption.

**Required governing artifacts before adoption:**

- A proposed successor System Record must define the new outcome, rule/version, authoritative state and source-of-truth boundary, actors and permissions, inputs/outputs and handoffs, support matrix, Normal/Degraded/Paused/Recovery modes, proof seams, monitoring, and predecessor-retirement conditions.
- A versioned Change and Legacy Record must bind current and proposed versions, rationale/evidence, scope, authority and approval; state a discoverable coverage query plus explicit unknown coverage; and preserve the original historical classifications rather than rewriting history.
- Classify every discoverable legacy case as `unaffected`, `compatible`, `auto-adapt`, `review-required`, `conflict`, `exempt`, or `unreachable/unknown`. For each non-unaffected cohort, record the exact proposed transformation or re-evaluation and its rationale. The proposed legacy disposition is therefore cohort-based migration/re-evaluation, with unresolved coverage held as `unreachable/unknown`, not blanket retroactive reclassification.

**Authority, incentives, friction, and evidence:** Only the authorized operator may approve the successor rule, authority expansion, cutover, or first activation. Models may interpret and propose classifications; deterministic mechanisms must own versions, IDs, workflow state, approvals, effect gates, and reconciliation. Do not make speed, volume migrated, or apparent agreement the outcome: measure classification quality against the accepted outcome, process fidelity, and burden/harm (including correction rate, exception volume, queue age, confusion, support load, and displaced work). Inspect incentives for agents and operators to over-classify, avoid ambiguous cases, or rubber-stamp review; use clear reversible defaults and bounded review queues rather than hidden pressure or repeated-memory demands. Every material assumption, ambiguity, decision, and proposed effect remains source-linked, reviewable, and human-readable.

**Operating and recovery contract:**

- **Normal:** Apply only the approved successor rule to eligible new cases; perform approved, bounded legacy cohorts through resumable batches with receipts and verification.
- **Degraded:** When capacity, a dependency, or information is limited, preserve originals and proposed classifications, continue only pre-authorized safe preparation or reversible cohort simulation, and surface a bounded exception queue. Do not guess a classification or widen authority.
- **Paused:** On missing approval, rule ambiguity, stale source, failed reconciliation, material conflict, or unsafe dependency, freeze live reclassification/cutover; preserve state and make the blocking condition discoverable.
- **Recovery:** Triage the bounded exception set; replenish/repair the missing input or capability; resolve stale assumptions and conflicts; revalidate the rule/version and authority; replay affected zero-effect simulations; verify the healthy baseline; then resume only the approved cohort. Recovery must not require reconstructing context or impose an unbounded backlog.

**Proof and transition obligations:** Before live effects, run zero-effect simulations using the production-intended retrieval, interpretation, decision, and composition path with all production effectors and durable/user-visible writes disabled. Simulate representative new cases and every legacy classification cohort, rendering the exact proposed classifications and transitions. Compare outcomes, fidelity, burden, conflicts, and unknown coverage; retain simulation receipts. Define cohort-level verification, reconciliation, coverage counts, rollback/repair actions, and a predecessor-rule rollback condition. A failed cohort, unresolved material ambiguity, or coverage gap pauses that cohort rather than silently applying the new rule.

**Workflow handoff:** Workflow may now coordinate bounded discovery of the authoritative rule and population, successor/legacy-record drafting, simulation, independent risk-proportionate review and separate resolution check, approval, pilot, rollout, monitoring, and possible rollback. It must not apply the rule, treat legacy coverage as complete, or claim the outcome complete before those obligations are met.
