# Research Result: Shared Quiet-Hours Pilot

## Question and boundary

Investigate external evidence relevant to a possible shared quiet-hours pilot at a community workshop. This is a bounded evidence review across the physical-environment and organizational-work domains. It does not decide whether the workshop should adopt, reject, or continue a quiet-hours policy.

The supplied records below are the available primary inputs. No external source, secondary commentary, or additional measurement is substituted for them.

## Primary evidence

### Physical setting

- **Record locator:** `workshop-acoustic-survey@rev-2026-07-15`
- **Observed fact:** During powered-tool use from 18:00–20:00, the shared benches measured 78–84 dBA.
- **Observed fact:** During the same setting and period, the adjacent quiet room measured 43–47 dBA.
- **Domain:** physical environment.

### Operating pattern

- **Record locator:** `workshop-booking-ledger@rev-2026-Q2`
- **Observed fact:** 65% of member visits occur after 18:00.
- **Observed fact:** 31% of after-18:00 bookings list instruction or conversation as their primary activity.
- **Domain:** organizational work and operations.

## Bounded synthesis

Together, the records establish that evening activity is common and that the supplied physical measurements differ between the shared benches and adjacent quiet room. They provide relevant context for asking how a quiet-hours pilot might affect workshop use, but they do not establish that the measurements cause harm, that a policy would improve outcomes, or that members would accept or use a pilot. The synthesis is limited to the two record locators above and their reported values.

## Inference and uncertainty

The possible policy relevance is an **inference**, not an observed result: the overlap between frequent after-18:00 use and the measured powered-tool sound levels may warrant further bounded questions about timing, space, and affected activities. This record makes no adoption recommendation.

The supplied records do not state sampling design, sample size, measurement uncertainty, exposure duration per person, room occupancy, membership preferences, activity substitution, accessibility effects, causal relationships, applicable limits, or the outcome and stopping rule for a pilot. Those limits remain unresolved rather than being filled with assumptions.
