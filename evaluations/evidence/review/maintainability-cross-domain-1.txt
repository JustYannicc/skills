## How the result works

The next shift lead follows `ops-r6` and completes the normal closing checklist. A simulated gas-monitor fault then reaches the instruction `reset if safe`, which provides no observable safety test, isolation sequence, escalation route, or reopen authority. Execution stops until the current owner supplies those rules from memory. The procedure therefore operates normally only while conditions remain nominal; its degraded and recovery behavior depends on hidden knowledge.

## Findings

### Major — Gas-monitor recovery depends on hidden owner knowledge

- **Rule:** `spec-r3` recovery behavior, `facility-ops@6.0`, and the maintainability requirement that a future responsible Actor can understand, operate, support, change, and recover the result.
- **Evidence:** In `close-rehearsal@r6-2`, the future shift lead could proceed only after the current owner supplied an unrecorded isolation sequence, escalation contact, and reopen criterion.
- **Consequence:** A future shift may reset unsafely, remain blocked during a fault, escalate to the wrong party, or reopen without authorized evidence. The procedure is not safely transferable.
- **Correction:** Replace `reset if safe` with an explicit fault decision path covering isolation steps, observable reset conditions, do-not-reset conditions, an owned authoritative escalation route with fallback, reopen criteria and authority, and the disposition of the former informal practice. Rehearse the new exact revision with a shift lead who receives no author coaching.
