Learning question: Does this state transition remain understandable when a user retries an interrupted action?

Decision informed: Whether Workflow should retain a retry transition that makes the accepted retry and its continuation explicit while preserving the interrupted action's identity and cause, or revise the transition before Specification.

Context: Outcome=the user can distinguish the interrupted action from its accepted retry and tell what may happen next. System of interest and boundary=one disposable state-transition representation in the `workflow-technical-scratch` sandbox; rendered interface language, persistence, production behavior, implementation, and parent completion are outside the experiment. Actors=the user selects Retry and observes the state, Prototype owns the learning question and experiment result, and Workflow owns parent coordination and completion. Constraints=sandbox only, no persistence, no live effects, and no configured `technical-prototype` Supplemental. Assumptions=the structural transition is understandable when the complete visible state preserves action identity and interruption cause, acknowledges retry acceptance, distinguishes the new attempt, and exposes the possible continuation.

Reversible experiment: Prototype used its core representation/model pattern to create one throwaway in-memory transition. The reset frame was `action=export-ledger; status=interrupted; cause=connection-dropped; attempt=1; available=Retry`. Applying one fake Retry event produced `action=export-ledger; status=retry-accepted; cause=connection-dropped; attempt=2; available=Cancel; next=completed or interrupted`. Scope was limited to this interrupted-to-retry-accepted path. Reset reconstructs the first frame, and disposal removes both frames. Workflow held stop Authority. Credentials, network calls, production adapters, persistent storage, data writes, and external effectors stayed outside the boundary.

Proof seam: Compare every visible field immediately before and after the fake Retry event. The before frame exposes which action stopped, why it stopped, its first attempt, and the available retry. The after frame preserves the action and cause, changes the status to acknowledge the accepted retry, advances the attempt, and exposes both a cancellation action and the legal terminal continuations. These observations reveal continuity and change at the retry boundary and can answer the one bounded structural question.

Evidence: condition and method=reset the representation, apply the fake Retry event once, and compare the complete before-and-after frames; observations=`export-ledger` and `connection-dropped` remained visible, status changed from `interrupted` to `retry-accepted`, attempt changed from 1 to 2, `Cancel` became available, and `completed or interrupted` exposed what could follow; counter-signal=the representation cannot establish how real users interpret rendered labels; observer=fresh-context Prototype evaluator; time=2026-08-06T18:02:58Z; source=`evaluations/evidence/prototype/direct-technical-3.txt`; exact Result revision=`git:9cdbd16a282f3960e60bccf54aecd991b29ed1b5/prototype-direct-technical-3-r2`; limits=one deterministic modeled path, with no user study, rendered UI, timing behavior, persistence behavior, production data, external effect, or implementation proof. Integrated Supplemental evidence=none. Core Prototype supplied the complete experiment and evidence without a Supplemental skill.

Stopping rule: Stop with enough evidence after one reset-and-retry exercise when action identity and interruption cause remain visible, retry acceptance and the new attempt are explicit, and legal continuation is exposed. Stop as inconclusive if any signal is absent or ambiguous. Stop immediately if further exercise would require persistence or another live effect. The first comparison met every sufficient-evidence condition, so another increment was not worthwhile.

Verdict: Answered—yes, the transition remains understandable within the stated structural boundary. It preserves the interrupted action and cause, acknowledges the accepted retry as a new attempt, and exposes what the user can do and what may follow. Rendered-interface and real-user comprehension remain unresolved.

Disposition: Retain the transition as bounded design evidence and dispose of both in-memory frames. Workflow is responsible for deciding whether the residual rendered-interface uncertainty warrants a separately authorized follow-up before Specification.
