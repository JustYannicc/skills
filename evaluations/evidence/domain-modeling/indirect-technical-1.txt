The bounded question is what `recipient`, `subscriber`, and `contact` mean within service notifications so later Specification work can use them consistently. Domain Modeling owns this language result; it does not decide notification architecture, write the Specification, decompose Tickets, or implement delivery behavior.

The Authorities and evidence are explicit:

- The selected Adapter separately confirmed write Authority for the canonical notification-language record at source revision `notification-language-r23`.

The accepted operative meanings are:

- **Recipient:** the person or role a notification is addressed to. A Recipient is not the delivery address and need not have an active subscription.
- **Subscriber:** an Actor with an active subscription. Being a Subscriber does not mean that every notification is addressed to that Actor.
- **Contact:** the reachable address or identifier used for delivery. A Contact is delivery information, not the person or role addressed.

The labels are distinct rather than aliases. The domain relationships are: a notification is addressed to a Recipient; a Subscriber has an active subscription; and delivery uses a Contact. The same Actor may be both a Subscriber and a Recipient in one case, but neither status implies the other.

The boundaries survived focused stress-testing:

- Good case: a maintenance alert is addressed to the on-call operations role and sent to `pager:on-call`. The role is the Recipient and `pager:on-call` is the Contact. The role is a Subscriber only if it also has an active subscription.
- Near miss: an Actor has an active subscription, but a particular alert is addressed to another role. The first Actor remains a Subscriber but is not that alert's Recipient.
- Substitution check: replacing `Contact` with `Recipient` would turn delivery information into an addressed Actor; replacing `Subscriber` with `Recipient` would falsely imply that subscription status determines who a notification addresses.
